How the sale arose

The property was sold on auction on 14 April 2026 for R3 400 000.00 to Passion Java, who concluded a sale agreement with the seller on the same date (“the original agreement”). This deed substitutes 34 Chartwell (Pty) Ltd as purchaser on those same terms. The purchaser confirms it is aware of the conditions of the original agreement, accepts that Passion Java's deposit stands to its credit, indemnifies the seller against any claim by Passion Java in respect of that deposit, and cedes the full R3 400 000.00 to the seller as security for payment of the full purchase consideration.

Matters requiring attention
1 · The seller has not signed

The acceptance block on page 18 — place, date, the seller's signature and both witnesses — is entirely blank. On the face of the document the purchaser's offer has not been accepted, and no binding sale has come into existence. This should be confirmed and the acceptance properly completed and dated before any further steps are taken, since several periods in the agreement run from the date of acceptance.

2 · Agricultural title restrictions and unconfirmed consent use

Clause 14.4 records that the holding must remain designated for agricultural, horticultural or livestock purposes with a minimum extent of 8 565 m². It may not be subdivided, and no portion may be sold, leased or alienated without the prior written approval of the local authority. It may not be held in joint ownership by two or more persons. Residential development is limited to one primary dwelling with necessary outbuildings, subject to a 15.24 m building setback from any road boundary.

Clause 14.5 records that consent use has been approved but the required payment has not been made, so the approval is not in effect, and that approved building plans could not be obtained from the seller or the Council. If the purchaser intends to develop or subdivide, this is the material risk in the transaction and should be raised in writing before transfer.

3 · Condition report is heavily qualified

The condition report at pages 25–26 is completed by hand and signed by Victoria Kinsella for the estate on 12 March 2026. It records that the executors have not lived at the property for many years and cannot disclose latent or patent defects; that the property has stood vacant since 2022 with maintenance not up to date; that there are gravel cracks and that consent use and occupancy certification were still being attended to. Read with the voetstoots clause, the purchaser carries the condition risk.

Drafting irregularities noted

None of these appear to affect the substance of the agreement, but they should be checked against the signed original and raised with the agent if the document is to be relied on:

• Clause 4 runs 4.1 and then 3.2 — the second should read 4.2.  • Clause 5.5 refers to “this clause 4” while sitting in clause 5.  • Clause 10 is headed Risk and Possession but its sub-clauses are numbered 9.1 to 9.3.  • Clause 19 appears twice — Sections 112 and 115 of the Companies Act, and again as Nominee.  • Clause 10.1 cross-refers to “clauses 1.1 and 1.3”, which on their content appear to be clauses 2.1 and 2.3.  • The trust and close corporation resolutions at pages 20 and 22 each number both items “1.” and “3.” respectively, and are struck through as not applicable.
